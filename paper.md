## Abstract

This study examines the possibility that India may benefit from establishing its own Parliamentary Office of Science and Technology (POST), following the UK model. Such an office could strengthen the scientific grounding and adaptability of legislation in areas such as artificial intelligence, climate change, and other fields with significant technical implications. Although India has NITI Aayog, it is distinct from an in-house parliamentary body analogous to the UK POST. It is important to note that an Indian POST would not be equivalent to NITI Aayog, which serves as a prominent government think tank.

## Introduction

The quality of legislation depends, in part, on the extent and depth of parliamentary deliberation. As scientific and technological developments accelerate, legislatures face increasing pressure to engage with complex evidence and emerging policy issues. To keep pace with rapid innovation and to make scientific knowledge more accessible to India’s Parliament, India may consider establishing its own POST, modeled on the UK Parliament. Such an institution could help ensure that parliamentary discussion is informed by evidence-based analysis and technical expertise. It may also contribute to reducing the influence of pseudoscientific claims or inaccurate assumptions in legislation and public administration by providing a permanent expert body dedicated to supporting Parliament’s understanding of scientific and technical issues.

## Methodology

This study advances a hypothesis based on existing public sources, including official websites of the UK Parliament and NITI Aayog. It does not report new empirical data or experiments.

## Discussion and Conclusion

Further research could assess the impact of the UK Parliament’s POST on legislative efficiency, policy quality, and public administration in the UK and the world. For the present purpose, however, this paper proposes that India may benefit from establishing its own Parliamentary Office of Science and Technology modeled on the UK example. An Indian POST should be understood as distinct from NITI Aayog.
